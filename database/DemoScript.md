# The Harbor Light

This short demo is a grounded investigative mystery for one player. The player is an investigator called to the old harbor district after a lighthouse keeper sends a strange message and vanishes before dawn.

## Scene 1: The Fogbound Pier

The investigator arrives at Graymouth Pier in the early evening. Rain taps against the warehouses, fishing boats knock against their ropes, and the lighthouse beam across the water is turning at an irregular rhythm. Constable Mara Vale waits beside a locked gate, a folded oilskin coat, and a damp crate marked by tidewater. The opening should make three natural approaches visible without solving them: question Vale, inspect the abandoned coat and crate, or ask to pass through the gate toward the lighthouse launch.

### Plot 1: Speak with Constable Vale

Constable Vale says lighthouse keeper Elias Rook sent one final radio message: "The light is counting backward." She found no blood at the pier, but the lighthouse launch is missing and the keeper's oilskin coat was left folded on a crate. She can answer basic questions about Rook, the coat, the launch, the lighthouse, and who last came to the pier. If questioned carefully or reassured, she admits the harbor bell rang five times at 3:17 a.m., even though the bell rope had been removed last winter.

If the investigator asks who else knew Rook's habits, Vale reluctantly mentions Jonah Mercer, owner of the old customs house. Mercer came to the pier two nights ago asking whether Rook had "fixed the order yet," then left before anyone could question him. If the player is unsure what to ask next, Vale can naturally offer: "You can question me, look over Rook's coat, or I can unlock the gate so you can see the launch mooring."

### Plot 2: Inspect the Pier

The pier boards are slick with rain. The folded oilskin coat, the crate beneath it, the locked gate, and the empty launch mooring are all visible and safe starting points. A basic inspection reveals the coat is dry inside despite the rain and smells faintly of lamp oil. A successful search can reveal fresh salt crystals arranged in five small arcs beside the crate. A hard or extreme success also notices that the crystals form the same pattern as the lighthouse lens shutters when they close. The locked gate can be opened by Vale, by force, or by a successful Locksmith-style action.

The gate should not stop the investigation. If Vale opens it, she begins to trust the investigator and answers one extra question honestly. If it is forced, someone in the harbor notices the noise before dawn. If it is opened quietly or the crate is checked closely, the investigator finds a customs house cargo stamp pressed into the wet crate beside Rook's coat. Any outcome should leave at least one clear next lead: the lighthouse launch route, the customs house stamp, or another question for Vale.

## Scene 2: The Lighthouse

The investigator reaches the lighthouse by launch. Inside, the stairwell smells of hot metal and seawater. The lamp room still burns, but the rotation mechanism is jammed by a brass notebook wedged between the gears.

### Plot 1: Read Rook's Notebook

The notebook contains Elias Rook's observations from the past week. He believed the light was no longer warning ships away from rocks; instead, it was signaling something beneath the harbor. The final page is written in a shaking hand: "Five flashes, five bells, five names. If the order is broken, it answers."

Two later lines are squeezed into the margin: "Mercer knows which name was removed. He heard the first wrong bell and lived." Beneath that, Rook has written, "Before dawn, one bell may reset the order. After dawn, the light starts counting again."

### Plot 2: Examine the Lamp Room

The lamp room windows are streaked with salt from the inside. A careful inspection reveals five names scratched into the metal frame: Rook, Vale, Mercer, Tain, and Sable. The name "Mercer" is crossed out. The lens can be stopped safely with a Mechanical Repair or DEX-style action, but a failure causes the light to flare and forces a SAN check.

If the investigator compares the scratched names with the notebook, they can see the names were meant to be read clockwise around the frame, matching the shutter positions. Mercer was not merely crossed out; his name was cut out of sequence.

## Scene 3: The Drowned Signal

When the lens is stopped or the pattern is understood, the harbor bell rings from below the water. The missing launch drifts back into view with Elias Rook inside, alive but barely conscious. He points toward the old customs house and whispers, "Mercer answered it first."

### Plot 1: Confront Mercer

Jonah Mercer, owner of the customs house, knows the five names are the current keepers of an old smuggling pact. He crossed out his own name because he tried to break the pact. If pressured with evidence from the notebook or the lamp room, Mercer admits that the bell marks each person who has heard the signal and that Rook is not the first to vanish.

Mercer inherited the pact from his father and tried to erase himself after the last winter disappearance. If confronted with the customs house stamp, the crossed-out name, or Vale's account of his late visit, he breaks more quickly. He says, "One bell. Five names. Always in order. That was how our fathers kept it sleeping. Five bells is not a warning. Five bells is an invitation."

### Plot 2: End the Signal

The signal can be ended by restoring the five-name order and ringing the harbor bell once at dawn, or by destroying the lens before it completes another backward count. The quieter solution saves Rook and leaves Mercer to confess. Destroying the lens ends the threat immediately, but the lighthouse burns and Graymouth loses its warning light for the winter.

To restore the order, the investigator must mark Mercer's name back into its proper place on the lamp room frame, speak or read the five names clockwise, and ring the old harbor bell once as dawn reaches the lens. The bell rope is gone, but the bell can be struck from the customs house winch chain, from the lighthouse launch hook, or by any practical tool the investigator brings to the pier.

## Knowledge

### NPC: Constable Mara Vale

Mara Vale is practical, exhausted, and protective of the harbor workers. She knows more than she first admits because her family name appears in old lighthouse records.

### NPC: Elias Rook

Elias Rook is the missing lighthouse keeper. He is cautious and methodical, and his notebook is the best source of confirmed information about the light.

### NPC: Jonah Mercer

Jonah Mercer owns the old customs house. He fears the signal and wants to survive more than he wants to tell the truth.

### Setting: Graymouth Harbor

Graymouth Harbor is an aging fishing port with a lighthouse, a fog bell, a locked pier gate, and an abandoned customs house. The harbor has a history of smuggling and disappearances during winter storms.

### Clue: Fivefold Pattern

The repeated number five connects the bell rings, salt arcs, scratched names, and lens shutter positions. The clue is obtained by comparing evidence from the pier and lighthouse.

### Clue: Ending the Signal

The signal is reset by one bell, five names, and the correct clockwise order before dawn. Rook's notebook gives the timing, the lamp room gives the order, and Mercer can explain the old rule if pressured with evidence.
