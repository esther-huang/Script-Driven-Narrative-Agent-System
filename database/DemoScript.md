# The Harbor Light

This short demo is a grounded investigative mystery for one player. The player is an investigator called to the old harbor district after a lighthouse keeper sends a strange message and vanishes before dawn.

## Scene 1: The Fogbound Pier

The investigator arrives at Graymouth Pier in the early evening. Rain taps against the warehouses, fishing boats knock against their ropes, and the lighthouse beam across the water is turning at an irregular rhythm. Constable Mara Vale waits near the locked gate and wants the investigator to determine whether the disappearance is an accident, a crime, or something stranger.

### Plot 1: Speak with Constable Vale

Constable Vale says lighthouse keeper Elias Rook sent one final radio message: "The light is counting backward." She found no blood at the pier, but the lighthouse launch is missing and the keeper's oilskin coat was left folded on a crate. If questioned carefully, she admits the harbor bell rang five times at 3:17 a.m., even though the bell rope had been removed last winter.

### Plot 2: Inspect the Pier

The pier boards are slick with rain. A successful search can reveal fresh salt crystals arranged in five small arcs beside the crate. A hard or extreme success also notices that the crystals form the same pattern as the lighthouse lens shutters when they close. The locked gate can be opened by Vale, by force, or by a successful Locksmith-style action.

## Scene 2: The Lighthouse

The investigator reaches the lighthouse by launch. Inside, the stairwell smells of hot metal and seawater. The lamp room still burns, but the rotation mechanism is jammed by a brass notebook wedged between the gears.

### Plot 1: Read Rook's Notebook

The notebook contains Elias Rook's observations from the past week. He believed the light was no longer warning ships away from rocks; instead, it was signaling something beneath the harbor. The final page is written in a shaking hand: "Five flashes, five bells, five names. If the order is broken, it answers."

### Plot 2: Examine the Lamp Room

The lamp room windows are streaked with salt from the inside. A careful inspection reveals five names scratched into the metal frame: Rook, Vale, Mercer, Tain, and Sable. The name "Mercer" is crossed out. The lens can be stopped safely with a Mechanical Repair or DEX-style action, but a failure causes the light to flare and forces a SAN check.

## Scene 3: The Drowned Signal

When the lens is stopped or the pattern is understood, the harbor bell rings from below the water. The missing launch drifts back into view with Elias Rook inside, alive but barely conscious. He points toward the old customs house and whispers, "Mercer answered it first."

### Plot 1: Confront Mercer

Jonah Mercer, owner of the customs house, knows the five names are the current keepers of an old smuggling pact. He crossed out his own name because he tried to break the pact. If pressured with evidence from the notebook or the lamp room, Mercer admits that the bell marks each person who has heard the signal and that Rook is not the first to vanish.

### Plot 2: End the Signal

The signal can be ended by restoring the five-name order and ringing the harbor bell once at dawn, or by destroying the lens before it completes another backward count. The quieter solution saves Rook and leaves Mercer to confess. Destroying the lens ends the threat immediately, but the lighthouse burns and Graymouth loses its warning light for the winter.

## Knowledge

### NPC: Constable Mara Vale

Mara Vale is practical, exhausted, and protective of the harbor workers. She knows more than she first admits because her family name appears in old lighthouse records.

### NPC: Elias Rook

Elias Rook is the missing lighthouse keeper. He is cautious and methodical, and his notebook is the best source of confirmed information about the light.

### NPC: Jonah Mercer

Jonah Mercer owns the old customs house. He fears the signal and wants to survive more than he wants to tell the truth.

### Setting: Graymouth Harbor

Graymouth Harbor is an aging fishing port with a lighthouse, a fog bell, a locked pier gate, and an abandoned customs house. The harbor has a history of smuggling and disappearances during winter storms.

### Clue: Fivefold Pattern

The repeated number five connects the bell rings, salt arcs, scratched names, and lens shutter positions. The clue is obtained by comparing evidence from the pier and lighthouse.
