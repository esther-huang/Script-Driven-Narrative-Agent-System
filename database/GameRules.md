# Lightweight d100 Investigation Rules

This project includes a compact, original rules summary for public demos and tests.
It is not a reproduction of any commercial tabletop role-playing rulebook. The goal is
to give the narrative agent enough shared vocabulary to adjudicate simple investigative
actions, dice checks, clues, stress, and consequences.

## Core Resolution

When an action has uncertainty and meaningful consequences, choose the most relevant
skill or characteristic and roll percentile dice. A result equal to or below the target
number succeeds. The Keeper should call for rolls only when failure would change the
scene, reveal a cost, create pressure, or force a new choice.

Use these broad success bands:

- Regular success: roll equal to or below the target.
- Hard success: roll equal to or below half the target.
- Critical success: roll very low, usually 01.
- Failure: roll above the target.
- Fumble: roll very high when the situation is risky, usually 96-100.

Do not make a single failed roll stop the story. A failed search can still reveal a clue
with a complication, a delay, a cost, a worse position, or a partial answer.

## Difficulty

Most checks use the listed target number. Use a hard check when the task is rushed,
dangerous, contested, poorly supported, or requires precision. Use an easy check when
the character has ideal tools, strong preparation, a clear lead, or low pressure.

When no exact skill applies, use a characteristic:

- STR for force, lifting, breaking, holding.
- CON for endurance, poison, cold, exhaustion, pain.
- DEX for speed, balance, stealthy movement, fine timing.
- INT for deduction, pattern recognition, tactical insight.
- POW for willpower, fear, focus, supernatural pressure.
- EDU for learned knowledge, research, professional recall.
- APP for first impressions and social presence.
- SIZ for body mass and physical scale.

## Investigation Flow

The Keeper should maintain momentum by making every scene answer at least one question:

- What is immediately visible?
- What can be learned through careful action?
- What new lead points to another person, place, object, or decision?
- What danger grows if the player hesitates or makes noise?

Clues should be layered. A basic clue may be available from the scene description, while
a successful roll reveals better context, a hidden connection, or a safer path. Important
core clues should not disappear because of one bad roll.

## Stress And Sanity

Use Sanity or stress checks when the character faces disturbing evidence, supernatural
implications, helplessness, isolation, or direct horror. A successful check means the
character keeps composure. A failed check means they lose confidence, time, clarity, or
stability.

For a lightweight public demo, treat sanity loss as narrative pressure rather than a full
separate subsystem. Good consequences include:

- The character hesitates and loses a chance to act first.
- The character fixates on one sensory detail.
- The next mental or social check is harder.
- The Keeper gives a vivid intrusive impression.
- The player must choose between retreat, denial, or pushing forward.

Magic points can be treated as a limited reserve of will or supernatural endurance. In a
short demo, avoid complex accounting unless the uploaded scenario explicitly asks for it.

## Combat And Physical Danger

Combat should be brief, risky, and consequence-focused. Use opposed rolls when two sides
act directly against one another, such as fighting, dodging, grappling, or fleeing.

Damage does not need detailed tables for the public demo. Use descriptive severity:

- Minor harm: painful but manageable.
- Moderate harm: limits movement or concentration.
- Major harm: forces immediate help, escape, or a serious cost.
- Deadly harm: can end the scene or character unless the player changes the situation.

The Keeper should telegraph danger before severe consequences. Give players a chance to
notice warning signs, take cover, negotiate, run, or solve the problem indirectly.

## Keeper Guidance

The agent should act like a fair Keeper:

- Ground responses in the current scene, plot, known clues, and player profile.
- Avoid inventing unrelated locations, objects, or lore that bypasses the script.
- Offer consequences, not dead ends.
- Preserve player agency by asking what they do next after describing outcomes.
- Keep secrets hidden until discovered, inferred, or dramatically revealed.
- Use dice when uncertainty matters, not for every small action.

When the player proposes a clever action, reward the intent even if the execution is risky.
When the player is stuck, provide a non-spoiler nudge based on already available clues.

## Player Character Baseline

Public demo investigators can be represented by characteristics, occupation skills, and
interest skills. Values around 40-50 are ordinary, 60-70 are competent, and 75+ indicates
a strong specialty. Derived values such as hit points, sanity, and move can be simple
approximations unless the scenario needs exact bookkeeping.

Use character background to color narration. A detective notices procedure, a journalist
notices story and sources, and a mechanic notices tools, wear, and practical failures.

## Uploaded Scenarios

For uploaded Markdown scenarios, the parsed scene and plot structure should remain the
primary source of truth. The rules summary supports adjudication, but the scenario decides
the mystery, locations, characters, threats, clues, and endings.
